Aligning energy and climate planning

How well do national energy scenarios and climate strategies actually align? This report analyses 114 planning documents to find out — where the two plans agree, where they diverge, and what drives better co-ordination between them.

Every LTES and LT-LEDS submitted by 57 countries and the European Union was reviewed — from Iceland to Vanuatu, spanning 20 energy transition indicators and plans published between 2016 and 2026.

Country deep dives

Three institutions, one trilateral memorandum of understanding

Legislation

The Net-Zero Emissions Accountability Act (2021) legally requires 5-year targets and reference to existing sectoral strategies.

Interministerial co-operation

ECCC, NRCan and CER share databases under a trilateral memorandum of understanding.

Capacity

Around 40 modellers across the two agencies, plus a cross-jurisdictional Energy Modelling Hub.

Responsibilities

Three large modelling teams on similar topics make clear role definition essential.

Challenge

A federal system means engaging provinces and municipalities equally on a national issue.

"Clearly outline each other's activities and assign responsibilities accordingly."— Canadian energy planners

COP25 turned political momentum into lasting institutional capacity

Legislation

The Framework Law on Climate Change (2022) makes the LT-LEDS the roadmap for the NDC, binding 10 ministries and all regional and municipal governments.

Interministerial co-operation

A legally mandated Interministerial Climate Change Team meets at least monthly.

Capacity

A dedicated Energy Ministry unit, in place since late 2024, runs both climate and long-term energy modelling.

Responsibilities

First Latin American country to submit an LT-LEDS (2021), aided by a World Bank-funded climate unit.

Challenge

Roughly 400 mitigation and adaptation plans across jurisdictional levels strain scheduling and follow-through.

"Framework legislation should mandate aligned update cycles, not just aligned content."— Chilean energy planners

A presidential mandate pulled sectoral ministries into climate planning

Legislation

A presidential mandate requires every sectoral ministry to align its targets with the national net-zero goal.

Interministerial co-operation

The Energy Ministry sent its own delegation to a COP for the first time in 2023.

Capacity

Planners recommend a standing interministerial team with shared, explicit deliverables.

Responsibilities

Ministries newly responsible for decarbonisation, like Transport and Housing, are still ramping up engagement.

Challenge

State-owned oil and gas companies' profit mandate sits in tension with the net-zero goal.

"Establish an interministerial team via presidential mandate, with deliverables as a shared responsibility."— Colombian energy planners

Energy and climate planning have been one process since the 1990s

Legislation

An updated 2022 climate law mandates net zero by 2035, giving planners policy consistency across changing governments.

Interministerial co-operation

Two ministries develop their strategies concurrently, sharing the same research institutes.

Capacity

VTT, Syke and Luke co-produce the evidence base for both the energy and the climate plan.

Responsibilities

Multi-stakeholder by original design, not by later co-ordination effort.

Challenge

National and EU-level reporting deadlines aren't fully synchronised.

"Adopt framework national climate legislation for policy consistency across administrations."— Finnish energy planners

Centralising two ministries into one, in 2022

Legislation

EU membership requires a National Energy and Climate Plan, subject to a lengthy Strategic Environmental Assessment.

Interministerial co-operation

One working group, chaired by one ministry, operates at both expert and political level.

Capacity

A single research consortium models both plans, using one model, REES-SLO.

Responsibilities

Before 2022, two separate ministries ran two working groups that rarely met.

Challenge

The mandatory environmental assessment has delayed the latest national plan by over a year and a half.

"Co-ordination improved once both plans sat under one ministry."— Slovenian energy planners

Conclusions

Five key findings and what they mean

Conclusions

Five key findings on the global status of alignment

5

Key findings

1

Up to 2030, energy and climate plans are equally ambitious. By 2050, climate plans are on average more ambitious. Two-thirds of countries published their climate plan at least a year before their energy plan — more recent energy plans may be catching up.

2

Alignment weakens beyond 2030, and long-term data comparability is limited. The average gap is 19% in 2030 but grows to 45% in 2050, with far fewer countries reporting comparable long-term data at all.

3

Sharing a publisher, cross-referencing, and using the same modelling tools correlate with better alignment. Sample sizes are small, but the pattern held across every institutional factor tested.

4

Success factors named by planners: a common analytical basis, strong political will, a multi-stakeholder approach, well-designed legislation, and advanced modelling capacity. No single country had all five — each offers a different practical model.

5

Multi-actor planning processes need clarity and regular exchange to work. Well-designed legislation, scheduled exchanges, formal co-operation mechanisms, and a clear map of who's responsible for what all featured repeatedly in planners' own advice.

Cutting across every finding

In countries where moving away from fossil fuels is the central political challenge, alignment between energy and climate plans shapes how transition costs and benefits are distributed across regions, sectors and workforces. Planning processes that build just transition considerations in from the outset are better placed to sustain political support — and attract investment in the communities most affected.

IRENA (2026), Long-Term Energy Scenarios and Low-Emission Development Strategies: Aligning energy and climate planning, International Renewable Energy Agency, Abu Dhabi.
